# Advanced track

The Advanced track is a faster recap for students who already have a mathematics
background, with further notions, up to the time we will have!
The lectures will be interactive, with examples made at the board, quick exercises to check what you understood.
We will do the correction all toghether, or I can help you individually if time allows it.

Two blocks, two sessions each: **linear algebra** (Sessions 1–2) and **analysis**
(Sessions 3–4). One idea per slide, carried by a picture, and one running example
— a small *participants × measures* data table — that comes back in every session.
Nothing from neuroscience is assumed: applications are two-sentence asides, never
prerequisites.

Session 5 is the **probability and statistics** session, taught by Ali Shiravand.

## Sessions

1. [**Linear Algebra I**]({{ '/advanced/session-1/' | relative_url }}) — vectors, span,
   independence, the dot product and projection, orthonormal bases and change of
   basis, matrices as transformations. *~110 min.*
2. [**Linear Algebra II**]({{ '/advanced/session-2/' | relative_url }}) — determinant,
   rank and collinearity, eigenvectors and diagonalisation, symmetric matrices and
   covariance, PCA, SVD. *~120 min.*
3. [**Analysis I**]({{ '/advanced/session-3/' | relative_url }}) — functions,
   derivatives, integrals, and a first differential equation. *~110 min.*
4. [**Analysis II**]({{ '/advanced/session-4/' | relative_url }}) — functions of
   several variables and the gradient, vector-valued functions and the Jacobian,
   systems of differential equations. *~120 min.*
5. [**Advanced Probability & Statistics**]({{ '/advanced/session-5/' | relative_url }}) —
   Bayesian inference, the multivariate Gaussian, maximum likelihood, the GLM, model
   comparison, and information theory. Taught by Ali Shiravand.

## How the sessions fit together

Session 1 builds everything out of two operations — adding vectors and scaling
them — and ends with the matrix as a transformation of space. Session 2 spends
that capital: rank, eigenvectors, PCA and the SVD are all questions about linear
combinations.

Session 3 does the same for analysis: what a function is, what a derivative and
an integral really say, and how to solve the one differential equation everything
else is built on. Session 4 adds the extra dimensions — several inputs, several
outputs, and systems of differential equations, which is exactly where the linear
algebra of Sessions 1–2 pays off a second time.
